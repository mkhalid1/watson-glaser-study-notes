# Timing and pacing

Pearson lists the Watson-Glaser as 40 items in 30 to 40 minutes, timed. The exact limit is set by the version your employer uses, and your invitation or the test's opening screen will tell you. This page shows how to pace for both ends of that range. The numbers below are our guidance, worked out from the published item count and time range.

## The budget

| Time limit | Average per item | Leaves a review buffer of 3 minutes at |
|---|---|---|
| 30 minutes | 45 seconds | about 40 seconds per item |
| 40 minutes | 60 seconds | about 55 seconds per item |

The average hides a big spread. Argument and assumption items are short. Inference and interpretation passages take longer to read, but each passage carries several items, so the reading time is shared.

## A pacing plan for 30 minutes

1. **Check the clock every ten items, not every item.** Aim to have answered 10 items by about 7 minutes, 20 by about 14, 30 by about 21. That leaves a few minutes in hand.
2. **Read each passage once, properly.** Skimming and rereading costs more than one careful read.
3. **Use a 60-second ceiling.** If an item is still unclear after a minute, pick your best answer, flag it if the platform lets you, and move on.
4. **Never leave a blank.** Your raw score is the number right, so a guess can only help.

## Where time usually goes

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Rereading inference passages for each statement | Note the key facts once: who, what, numbers, and what is opinion |
| Arguing with the facts in argument items | Say "Suppose that is correct" and judge weight only |
| Doing interpretation sums in your head twice | Write the figures down the first time |
| Agonising between two inference labels | Default to the more cautious label: PT over T, ID over PT |
| Treating deduction like a puzzle | Rewrite premises in "All A are B" form and look for one counterexample |

## Practise against the clock

Untimed practice teaches the rules. Timed practice teaches you what 45 seconds feels like. Do at least one full timed run before the real test, ideally two, with a day between them. The [free Watson-Glaser practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/watson-glaser?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=watson-glaser) is timed and covers all five sections.

Source for the item count and time range: [Pearson TalentLens](https://www.talentlens.com/watson-glaser.html).
