# For law applicants: training contracts and vacation schemes

If you are applying for a training contract, a vacation scheme or a solicitor apprenticeship at a UK firm, there is a good chance you will meet the Watson-Glaser. This page covers what changes for you compared with a general candidate.

## Where it sits in a typical process

Firms design their own processes and change them most cycles, so always read the firm's own careers page. As one published example, [Clifford Chance's London hiring page](https://jobs.cliffordchance.com/how-we-hire-london) describes:

- an online application form
- the Clifford Chance Immersive Online Assessment, which the firm says lasts around 45 to 60 minutes and should be completed within four days (including weekends)
- the Watson Glaser test, taken after the online assessment, again with four days (including weekends) to complete it
- deadlines for both tests sent by email once you submit your application

The same firm's [FAQ](https://jobs.cliffordchance.com/faq-london) says a passed Watson Glaser score is saved for two recruitment cycles, and that its own practice test can be taken as often as you like without the firm seeing the results.

Questions such as "Does CMS use the Watson-Glaser?" or "Does DLA Piper use it?" come up often. The only reliable answer is the firm's own application page or its graduate recruitment team, because the test and the cut-off can change between cycles.

## What this means for your timing

1. **Apply early in the window.** The tests usually follow the application, so applying near the deadline squeezes your test window. Clifford Chance's page says that candidates who apply in the final week before the deadline have less time to complete the online tests.
2. **Prepare before the link arrives.** If a firm gives you a few days, that is not the time to learn the rules from scratch. Work through the [7-day plan](study-plans.md#7-day-plan) before you submit your first application.
3. **Ask about adjustments early.** If you need extra time or other adjustments, request them when you apply. Clifford Chance asks applicants needing adjustments to apply before the deadline.
4. **Check whether a score carries over.** Some firms keep a passed score. Others may ask you to sit again.

## Why law firms care about these skills

Each subtest maps to something you will do as a trainee:

| Subtest | In practice |
|---|---|
| Inference | Reading a witness statement or a set of facts and separating what is proved from what is suggested |
| Recognising assumptions | Spotting what a client instruction, a contract term or an opponent's letter quietly relies on |
| Deduction | Applying a definition or a condition exactly as written, no wider and no narrower |
| Interpretation | Not overstating what a report, survey or piece of evidence shows |
| Evaluation of arguments | Summarising both sides fairly and dropping the points that do not bear on the issue |

Pearson's efficacy report also cites research comparing Watson-Glaser scores with final grades on the Bar Professional Training Course in England and Wales, which found a positive link.

## Habits that trip up law students in particular

- **Using legal knowledge.** You may know how a contract or a statute works. The test only cares what the passage says. If a passage describes a law differently from reality, accept the passage.
- **Arguing every point.** Law training rewards finding the counter-argument. In the arguments section, judge each argument on its own weight: having a good reply does not make it weak.
- **Over-reading "beyond reasonable doubt".** In interpretation it simply means the paragraph clearly establishes the conclusion. Do not import the criminal law meaning and look for every possible doubt.
- **Treating "some" as "only some".** In deduction, "some" can mean all.

## Practise before you apply

The [free Watson-Glaser practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/watson-glaser?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=watson-glaser) gives you a full timed run. Take one before your first application goes in, so the real link does not catch you cold.
