# 10-minute diagnostic

Ten original items, two from each subtest, to show you where to start. Set a timer for 8 minutes, answer everything without looking anything up, then check your answers and read the table at the bottom.

These items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the Watson-Glaser or any publisher's materials.

If you already know the rules of each section, skip this and take the [free full-length Watson-Glaser practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/watson-glaser?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=pvdq3r&utm_content=watson-glaser) instead. A full timed run gives a better picture of pacing.

## Part A: Inference

Rate each statement True, Probably True, Insufficient Data, Probably False or False, using only the facts and widely shared common knowledge.

> A café next to a university campus stays open until midnight during the exam period in May and June. Outside the exam period it closes at 6 p.m. The owner says the café's takings between 9 p.m. and midnight in May and June are higher than in any other three-hour period of the year.

**D1.** The café is open at 8 p.m. on an ordinary day in March.

**D2.** Many of the café's late-evening customers in May and June are students.

## Part B: Recognising assumptions

Is each assumption made or not made?

> "Book your train tickets today, because prices go up closer to the date of travel."

**D3.** The listener is planning to travel by train.

**D4.** The train will sell out before the date of travel.

## Part C: Deduction

Treat the premises as true without exception. Does each conclusion follow?

> All of the firm's offices in Scotland use the new billing system. The firm's Aberdeen office does not use the new billing system.

**D5.** The firm's Aberdeen office is not in Scotland.

**D6.** Every office that uses the new billing system is in Scotland.

## Part D: Interpretation

Does each conclusion follow beyond reasonable doubt?

> In 2025 a university law faculty received 3,000 applications and made 900 offers. In 2026 it received 3,600 applications and made 900 offers.

**D7.** A smaller share of applications received an offer in 2026 than in 2025.

**D8.** Offers were harder to get in 2026 because applicants were weaker.

## Part E: Evaluation of arguments

Treat each argument as true. Strong or weak?

> Should UK universities be required to record every lecture and make the recordings available to enrolled students?

**D9.** Yes. Students who are ill or who have caring responsibilities could keep up with their course.

**D10.** No. Some lecturers dislike hearing their own voice on recordings.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, D1 to D10</summary>

**D1: False.** Outside the exam period the café closes at 6 p.m. March is outside the exam period, so it is closed at 8 p.m. The passage contradicts the statement.

**D2: Probably True.** The café is next to a campus and opens late only in the exam period. It is common knowledge that students study late during exams. Not True, because the passage never says who the customers are.

**D3: Made.** Advice to book train tickets only makes sense for someone travelling by train.

**D4: Not made.** The reason given is price, not availability. The advice works even if seats never run out.

**D5: Follows.** Every Scottish office uses the system. Aberdeen does not, so it cannot be one of the Scottish offices. Your knowledge that Aberdeen is a Scottish city does not count: you must accept the premises.

**D6: Does not follow.** This reverses the first premise. Offices elsewhere could use the system too.

**D7: Follows.** 900 of 3,000 is 30%. 900 of 3,600 is 25%. The share fell.

**D8: Does not follow.** The paragraph gives numbers of applications and offers only. A lower offer rate is explained by more applications for the same number of places, not by weaker applicants.

**D9: Strong.** Helping students who cannot attend to keep up is an important benefit and directly about recording lectures.

**D10: Weak.** A lecturer's discomfort is a minor point next to students' access to teaching.

</details>

## What your result means

Count your correct answers in each part.

| Part | Your score | If you scored 0 | If you scored 1 | If you scored 2 |
