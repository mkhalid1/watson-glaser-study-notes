# Frequently asked questions

These are the questions people search for most about the Watson-Glaser, drawn from Google's "People also ask" results for UK searches in November 2026. Answers stick to what the publisher or employers state, and say so when the answer is our guidance.

## What is the Watson-Glaser test?

A timed critical thinking test published by Pearson TalentLens. You get 40 items in 30 to 40 minutes across five subtests: inference, recognising assumptions, deduction, interpretation and evaluation of arguments. Results are reported as percentiles for three areas: Recognise Assumptions, Evaluate Arguments and Draw Conclusions. See the [test overview](test-overview.md).

## What is a critical thinking test?

A test of reasoning from written information: judging what follows from facts, what is assumed, and which arguments carry weight. It does not test general knowledge. The Watson-Glaser is the best known example, and Pearson describes critical thinking as looking at a situation from several perspectives while separating facts from opinions and assumptions.

## How long is the Watson-Glaser test?

Pearson lists it as 40 items in 30 to 40 minutes, timed. The exact limit for your sitting is set by the version your employer uses and is shown in your invitation or on the test's start page. See the [timing guide](timing.md).

## Is there a pass mark?

No universal one. Your raw score out of 40 is turned into a percentile against a norm group that the employer chooses, and each employer sets its own threshold. Numbers quoted online, such as 75% or the 80th percentile, are not published by Pearson. See [how scoring works](scoring.md).

## What is the average Watson-Glaser score?

By definition, an average performance against a norm group is around the 50th percentile. Because the raw score needed for any percentile depends on the norm group, there is no single "average raw score" that applies everywhere. Pearson's own example: 35 out of 40 against its Manager norm is the 86th percentile.

## What is a good Watson-Glaser score?

Good means above the threshold of the employer you are applying to, against the norm group they use, which you usually will not be told. As study guidance, if you can score around 32 or more out of 40 on well-made timed practice, you have a comfortable margin. Also watch your three area scores, not just the total.

## Which law firms use the Watson-Glaser?

Law firms use it for training contract and vacation scheme applications. Clifford Chance, for example, describes the test on its [London hiring page](https://jobs.cliffordchance.com/how-we-hire-london). Firms change their assessments between cycles, so the only reliable source for any firm is its own careers page.

## Does CMS (or DLA Piper, or another firm) use the Watson-Glaser?

Check the firm's own application page or ask its graduate recruitment team. Lists on third-party sites go out of date quickly, and some firms use other critical thinking or situational judgement tests instead. See the [law applicants guide](law-applicants.md).

## Is the Watson-Glaser timed?

Yes. Pearson lists it as timed, with a 30 to 40 minute limit for 40 items. Individual firms may also set a deadline of a few days to complete the test after you receive the link: Clifford Chance gives four days including weekends.

## Can ChatGPT answer the Watson-Glaser test?

Using an AI tool or anyone else's help during a live test almost certainly breaks the test terms you agree to, and firms treat that as misconduct. It is also a poor bet: the score is meant to predict how you reason, and the firms that use it go on to assess you in person. Use AI tools, if at all, to explain practice items to you while you study, and check their reasoning against the rules on these pages. Our general guidance, not a statement from Pearson.

## How do I pass a critical thinking test?

1. Learn the exact rule for each question type. Most lost marks come from applying the wrong standard.
2. Work only from the passage. Leave your knowledge and opinions out.
3. Practise under time pressure.
4. Review every miss and name the rule you broke.

The [study plans](study-plans.md) turn this into a day-by-day schedule.

## Can I retake the Watson-Glaser?

That is up to each employer. Some keep a passed score for later applications: Clifford Chance saves a passed score for two recruitment cycles, according to its [FAQ](https://jobs.cliffordchance.com/faq-london). Check the rules of each firm you apply to.

## Is there an official practice test?

Yes. Pearson TalentLens offers a [free practice critical thinking test](https://www.talentlens.com/practice-tests.html) in English and Dutch. Pearson says it is not a live test and cannot be scored in a meaningful way, but it shows the question types and gives you preparation advice. Some employers, such as Clifford Chance, also offer a practice version to their applicants.

## Does the Watson-Glaser use the same questions for everyone?

No. Watson-Glaser III draws each candidate's questions from a large item bank. Pearson says each test is built to a similar mix of difficulty and topics, and small differences in difficulty are allowed for in the scoring.

## In which languages is it available?

Pearson lists UK English, US English, French, French Canadian, Dutch, Spanish and Latin American Spanish.
