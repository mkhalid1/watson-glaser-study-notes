# Sources

Every fact in these notes about the test itself comes from the publisher or from an employer's own page. Each source below was checked live on 24 November 2026. If something has changed, please [open an issue](https://github.com/mkhalid1/watson-glaser-study-notes/issues).

## Publisher: Pearson TalentLens

| Source | Used for |
|---|---|
| [Watson-Glaser Critical Thinking Appraisal product page](https://www.talentlens.com/watson-glaser.html) | 40 items, 30 to 40 minutes timed, languages, uses, the five subtests, item banking and equivalence, available reports, norms |
| [Watson-Glaser efficacy report, 2020](https://www.talentlens.com/content/dam/school/global/Global-Talentlens/uk/CriticalThinking/CTHub/Pearson_Efficacy_Watson-Glaser_2020.pdf) | RED model, 12/12/16 split of items by area, raw score 0 to 40, norm groups and the 35 out of 40 example, neutral and controversial scenarios, history and editions, research on course grades |
| [Free practice critical thinking test](https://www.talentlens.com/practice-tests.html) | Availability of the official practice test, and that it is unscored |
| Watson-Glaser UK Edition practice test booklet, published by Pearson Assessment ([copy hosted by Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2018/08/wg_practice.pdf)) | The answer rules for each subtest: the five inference labels, the use of common knowledge, "some" meaning possibly all, the beyond-reasonable-doubt standard, and the strong and weak argument definitions. This is an older, fixed-form UK edition, not Watson-Glaser III. We use it only for the rules, which we describe in our own words. None of its questions or examples appear here. |
