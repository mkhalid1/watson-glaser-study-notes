# Glossary

Terms you will meet on the test, in score reports and in these notes.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Argument (strong)** | An argument that is both important and directly related to the question, judged as if it were true. |
| **Argument (weak)** | An argument that is not directly related to the question, or is minor, or deals only with a trivial side of it. |
| **Assumption** | Something a speaker takes for granted without saying it. On the test, "made" means the statement depends on it. |
| **Beyond reasonable doubt** | The standard in interpretation and for "True" in inference: clearly established by the passage, even if not logically certain. |
| **Common knowledge** | Facts practically everyone shares. Allowed when judging Probably True or Probably False in inference. Specialist knowledge is not. |
| **Conclusion follows** | In deduction: must be true if the premises are true. In interpretation: follows beyond reasonable doubt. |
| **Controversial scenario** | A passage on a political, economic or social issue, included to test reasoning when you have strong views. |
| **Deduction** | The subtest where conclusions must follow necessarily from the premises. |
| **Denial test** | Our name for a quick check on assumptions: imagine the assumption is false and see whether the statement still makes sense. |
| **Development Report** | A Pearson report that helps build a learning plan for critical thinking skills. |
| **Draw Conclusions** | One of the three RED score areas. Covers inference, deduction and interpretation. |
| **Evaluate Arguments** | One of the three RED score areas. Covers the evaluation of arguments subtest. |
| **Inference** | The subtest where you rate statements from True to False. Also, in general, a conclusion drawn from facts. |
| **Insufficient Data (ID)** | The inference label for when the facts give no basis for leaning either way. |
| **Interpretation** | The subtest where you judge whether a conclusion follows beyond reasonable doubt from evidence. |
| **Interview Report** | A Pearson report with questions for a structured interview on critical thinking. |
| **Item** | A single question. The test has 40. |
| **Item bank** | A large pool of questions from which each candidate's test is drawn. Used by Watson-Glaser III. |
| **Neutral scenario** | A passage on a low-emotion topic, such as weather, science or ordinary business. |
| **Norm group** | The comparison group your raw score is measured against, such as graduates or managers. |
| **Percentile** | The share of the norm group you scored the same as or higher than. |
| **Premise** | A statement you must accept as true in a deduction item. |
| **Probably False (PF)** | The inference label for when the facts make a statement more likely false than true. |
| **Probably True (PT)** | The inference label for when the facts make a statement more likely true than false. |
| **Profile Report** | Pearson's main score report: overall percentile plus a percentile for each RED area. |
| **Raw score** | The number of items you got right, from 0 to 40. |
| **RED model** | Pearson's model of critical thinking: Recognise assumptions, Evaluate arguments, Draw conclusions. |
| **Recognise Assumptions** | One of the three RED score areas. Covers the recognising assumptions subtest. |
| **"Should" (in argument questions)** | In the UK edition, asks whether the action would promote the general welfare of people in the United Kingdom. |
| **"Some" (in deduction)** | At least one, and possibly all. |
| **Stanine** | A standard score on a nine-point scale, 1 to 9. |
| **Sten** | A standard score on a ten-point scale, 1 to 10. |
| **T-score** | A standard score with a mean of 50 and a standard deviation of 10. |
| **Training contract** | The period of recognised training at a law firm that leads to qualification as a solicitor in England and Wales. Many firms use the Watson-Glaser when recruiting for it. |
| **Vacation scheme** | A short paid work placement at a law firm, often a route to a training contract. |
| **Watson-Glaser III** | The current edition of the test, which uses an item bank. |
